# Table role

Therica Ashtongue is an **NPC**, commonly voiced by the **GM (Steel)**.[^therica-role] Her participation as a traveling companion does not make her a player character. See the player roster.

# Identity and family

The user identifies Therica as the daughter of Highhelm’s former king.[^therica-dictation] The primer names that king **Iolas Ashtongue IV**.[^primer] The dictation’s “Yolis Ashtong” is matched contextually to that name by his role as Highhelm’s former king; the original words remain preserved.

This page uses **Therica Ashtongue**, the supplied portrait filename spelling.[^portrait] Dictation uses “Therica Ashtong”; this is treated as the same character, without registering the dictated variant as an approved alias.

# Condition described by the user

The user describes a condition resembling a severe allergy to cold iron, similar to the condition of “Morgane” from Campaign 1, with portions of Therica’s face taking on an almost grayscale appearance.[^therica-dictation] “Allergy” and “grayscale” are descriptive comparisons; no exact diagnosis, mechanical effect or shared underlying cause has been established. The Campaign 1 name’s canonical spelling remains under review (Morgaen is a source-supported candidate).

# September 28 summons

After some time following the sun event, Therica summoned the party, described the history of their relationship with the Court of Conscience and began outlining a request for a journey. The historical account remains incomplete; a subsequent note supplies the airship investigation proposal.[^therica-dictation]

# Airship proposal

Therica proposed crossing the ocean to **Eok**, to the city called **Lastings** in the dictation, and asking the Court of Conscience’s leadership to lend, sell or otherwise provide access to an airship. She explained that Rios lacks airship technology while the Court has it. Her goal was to obtain airship access or technology so the party could investigate the sun event and the unidentified comet/meteor, and assess a possible threat to Dochas Ùr, Rios or the planet.[^airship-dictation]

The user identifies Dochas Ùr’s **Pillars** as the original adventuring party from Campaign 1. In Therica’s briefing, accounts attributed to the Pillars described flying much higher than expected, suggesting that the world’s apparent sky, roof or ceiling might be reachable by airship. This is reported evidence and an inference behind the proposed investigation; no sky boundary or airship reaching it has been directly established.[^airship-dictation]

See [mission record](../quests/airship-investigation.md).

# Joining the voyage

The party accepted Therica’s mission. Emeric persuaded her to accompany them with a very high roll, and she agreed immediately; neither the exact roll nor the skill used is supplied.[^departure-dictation]

Therica feared for her position and the leadership of Dochas Ùr. She expected House Glaren to make a bid for leadership while the party was away and thought it likely the House would be in charge upon their return. This is her prediction, not an accomplished takeover. The user identifies Sygar as an ostracized son of that House who renounced his family and became one of the original Pillars.[^departure-dictation] House Glaren’s spelling remains provisional because this is software dictation.

[^departure-dictation]: User retrospective note, heading “acceptance, companions and departure briefing”: paragraphs 1–3 (acceptance, persuasion and political concern), paragraph 4 (research, captain and Saga), paragraph 5 (departure and incomplete precept briefing).

# Affliction clarified

The user further describes Therica as cursed with an affliction similar to Fae, causing an extreme allergy or sensitivity to **cold iron or wrought metal**. This expands the earlier cold-iron description. The curse’s cause and precise mechanics remain unspecified.[^sea-dictation]

# Session 4 development

Therica aids the chase and secures the chain to the ship’s steering mast while Cole boards the whale. An articulated wooden woman appears and speaks during the return-form descriptions, likely Therica by companion and turn-order context, but not explicitly named there; the earlier clay figure is not silently reconciled. She is later described as searching the wreck.[^s004]
