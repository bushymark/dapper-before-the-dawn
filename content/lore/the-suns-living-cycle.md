# Haskel’s explanation

Haskel shares restricted cosmological knowledge in an effort to answer the Drifters’ fears: the sun is a gathering of living creatures, described as **sun salamanders**, which emerge from the sea, cross the sky together, and return to the water. He says the sun did not explode when the meteor passed through it; its creatures dispersed to dodge the impact.[^s008-yt]

In this account, they become fish at sunset, feed in separate schools, travel through the seas and reassemble to rise again. Their exact route, timetable, intelligence and vulnerability are not measured here. Haskel believes the account and a successful knowledge check supports his understanding; the party has not independently observed that complete cycle. Dayne has never heard this explanation, while others ask questions or express doubts.[^s008-yt]

# The sacred ecosystem

This account connects the first precept’s sacred sky and sea to protecting a fragile ecosystem: eating fish risks consuming part of the solar life cycle. Haskel warns that exploitation or pollution could have catastrophic consequences. The lesson is not ordinary public doctrine shared by every Citizen.[^s008-yt]

A familiar explanation attributes changing day and night lengths to conflict between the Summer and Winter Fey courts. It is not a statement that sunrise follows a fixed real-world schedule.[^s008-yt]

# What remains unanswered

Even if the dispersed sun was evading impact, the reason for the meteor and the separate airship strike remains unexplained. The party still wants to investigate. No one demonstrates a small artificial sun, certifies that Rios is safe or proves that the impacts were deliberate attacks.[^s008-yt]
