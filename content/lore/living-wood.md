# Ships with a preferred pilot

Living wood forms Whipperwill’s hull. In the older Court ships, it recognizes a pilot and resists someone else’s attempt to steer; the wheel itself can work against an unfamiliar pilot. The technology became associated with the Bloom and fell out of favor as brass, iron and steam became more accepted. This is the setting history given during Dayne’s attempt to understand the damaged vessel.[^s009-repairs]

# Dayne’s connection

Dayne approaches Whipperwill gently and binds its detached wheel to his arm. The vessel recognizes him as its pilot, recalling his help during its distress. In a private moment, broken timber bends toward his hand and begins strengthening. The response helps the living-wood repair track; it does not finish the hull or restore lift by itself.[^s009-repairs]

# Boats beneath the mine

Maren finds smaller wooden boats beside Rustwater Mine’s lake. Their grain runs seamlessly, with no nails or joins, and matches the airship’s wood. Their weak lantern-light communicates in an elemental manner he can recognize through Primordial. The party does not board them or determine whether the same pilot bond is required.[^s009-boats]

The mine’s growing metal and stone raise an analogy, but a shared origin is not established. [Living stone and metal](living-stone-and-metal.md).
