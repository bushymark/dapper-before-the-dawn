# Plots and dawn

A **plot** means territory covered by resurrection magic, rather than a graveyard allotment. Plots begin in cities and can be extended into rural areas. The magic returns eligible dead people at **dawn**; Eok’s day and night lengths are variable. Resurrection normally restores the person where their body lies. Outside the covered area, recovery depends on someone finding and rescuing the body before it becomes unusable.[^s005]

A **victus** carries a separated portion of the person’s soul and identifying information. The separation is made at birth. It makes restoration easier even after severe bodily damage and can extend how long recovery remains possible. It does not independently guarantee revival beyond the spell’s coverage. The Court maintains records of identities and victus objects, allowing objects to be located when needed. The Citizens’ birth practices and Rios’s ordinary resurrection differ; neither is replaced here by generic Pathfinder rules.[^s005]

# Severance and investigation

For someone accused or convicted of a crime, the Court may sever the connection while taking their body for questioning. Session 5 discusses recovering bodies before the next dawn as a route to return; Drifters without victus objects may need a proper plot or greater proximity. The actual recovery has not yet been tested.[^s005]

The GM clarifies that the party’s partial physical and magical relocation interfered with ordinary resurrection, and **no dawn has occurred since their return**. This does not establish that exactly three weeks passed without dawn; that suggested wording is corrected during the discussion.[^s005]

# Strain at the boundary

[Recknar](../characters/mysterious-blacksmith.md) repeatedly killed two captive messengers to exhaust a marginal rural plot. He says its boundary receded by miles, leaving the captives permanently dead and potentially exposing distant Court barracks. The bodies support the immediate outcome; the full extent and any remote casualties remain unverified.[^s005]

The forest’s starving wolves show another cost: creatures can die of hunger and return without their underlying food shortage being solved. The party plans to return with help.[^s005]

# Origin of the spell

A Forged from Rios named **[Yan Savos](../characters/yan-savos.md)** reportedly gave the Court this magic in exchange for a fleet of nine dreadnought-scale skyships. The Court tested it for two or three years before the first plot, checking possible changes to people and harm to their souls. He departed into the astral sea and has not returned in the account given.[^s005]

# Session 6 — recovery limits and revised assumptions

The assumption that officials definitely severed the party’s links is revisited: the Court appears puzzled by the failure to revive them, and any severance could have been undone for questioning. This is a later assessment, not proof of the exact procedure used. It qualifies the Session 4 account without erasing it.

The GM’s final clarification requires dawn within covered territory, body–soul proximity and recovery of the Citizens’ victus objects. The plot reaches from Alora toward the smith’s forge and stops there. Escape beyond it with dead companions does not guarantee return. Temporary-form expiry and violent destruction are not fully interchangeable, and no collapse experiment is performed. Possible capture/Fall consequences are discussed conditionally, not suffered on-screen.[^s006]
