# The heist takes shape

The group remains in [Smidgen’s](../characters/smidgen.md) refuge in [Alora Valley](../places/alora-valley.md). Maren’s theatrical approach becomes a two-part diversion: **Koris, Maren, Emeric and Therica** will handle disguises, performance and fireworks; **Cole, Haskel, Dayne and Elias** will retrieve the bodies. They intend to draw away enough guards to make the recovery possible, not assume every soldier will abandon the stage. Catapulting the coffins, burying the bodies and posing as an academic envoy are considered but not carried out.[^s006-plan]

A successful recall identifies a better alternative to carrying the heavy shells: [the coffins](../lore/court-body-containment-coffins.md) can be opened by tracing a Court symbol. They resist ordinary physical attacks and lower-level magic, but sonic attacks can destroy them. The party has not opened any of them yet.[^s006-coffins]

# Deterioration and the gauntlet’s answer

Emeric’s thumb begins hanging from his temporary hand; Therica also complains of pieces coming loose. Emeric admits uncertainty about whether more sacrifice might be needed to return everyone. He requests the group’s consent before questioning [Apherion](../characters/apherion.md), and promises to relay the response without editing it.[^s006-answer]

Apherion broadly agrees with their understanding but cannot say what complete collapse of the temporary forms would do. It proposes testing death on a less-useful companion and then on the crocodile-like Koris, whom it believes returns when killed. Emeric refuses to trade anyone’s life for information and reassures Koris. No such test or further bargain is performed.[^s006-answer]

The fear shifts toward awakening in guarded coffins at dawn. The earlier assumption that the Court definitely severed their links is questioned: the Court appears puzzled by their failed revival, and any severance could have been reversed for questioning. This later assessment does not establish precisely what officials did.[^s006-severance]

# Haskel’s duty and the party’s trust

Haskel refuses to transfer supervision of Elias to Dayne, even when Dayne offers to take responsibility for violations. He fears imprisonment and loss of the station he worked to obtain. Dayne’s scholarly standing may influence important people but does not give him power to suspend the Court’s rules. Cole and the Drifters argue that their best chance is cooperation and try to keep the Citizens’ families and reputations out of additional danger.[^s006-duty]

Haskel ultimately agrees to release the bodies if the party promises **no further murder**. Shelter in Rios is offered if the Citizens lose their place at home; Therica recalls a Forged founding Pillar as evidence that Forged can belong there. Haskel’s willingness depends on avoiding war between their nations. The discussion raises that more than a month may have passed; the party does not know what became of the threatened change in Dochas Ùr’s leadership.[^s006-agreement]

# Costumes and a dangerous diversion

Koris, Maren, Emeric and Therica reach [the theater tent](../factions/theater-of-the-mind-flayer.md). Inside are professional wigs, costumes, makeup and pyrotechnics. Therica strikes a page with a chair, renders him unconscious and conceals him beneath wigs and a dress. The page is not killed.[^s006-theater]

Maren quietly transports supplies back to Elias while Emeric assembles a vampire-wizard disguise for him: long black hair, theatrical clothing, a floating spellbook and false facial hair and fangs. The stage effects include burning-sword strips, heatless colored hellhounds and smoke devices called “egg.” Elias combines existing materials into a larger diversion with Cole’s assistance, and Koris stores the prepared device. Its decisive crafting/activation check is deferred; **no fireworks are set off**.[^s006-supplies]

Elias describes this as known work rather than a new invention. The GM nevertheless rules that the modification could be evidence that he is disregarding his sentence. In a private conversation, Haskel questions his ambition and intentions, then permits him to proceed. That permission is not a Court pardon or a ruling that the construction is lawful.[^s006-invention]

# A changing scene at the stage

The eight containers stand upright beside an open-air stage, with guards nearby and more around town. Binders continue rituals and transform potted shrubs into angry skeletal shapes. A grain-tower bell warns that dawn is approaching; Smidgen says notice comes from an eastern settlement. The reason for this advance warning remains unexplained.[^s006-stage]

The general studies a large map as coordinates are read out and possessions are removed from a table. A young page looks concerned. The general’s attention shifts from the coffins to this other issue, but the party does not know what the news means.[^s006-map]

# Where the session leaves the party

The plan changes from transporting loaded coffins to opening them and loading the bodies onto the cart. Haskel can trace the symbol; Elias intends to copy it after observing him. Communication between the teams will use existing mental links and relays. Neither copying the symbol nor opening a coffin has happened.[^s006-end]

The group is poised to act when play stops. The following table discussion stresses that fighting near the recovery team is still likely. Resurrection requires dawn within covered territory and body–soul proximity; the Citizens’ **victus objects must also be recovered**. Fleeing beyond the plot with dead companions will not automatically restore them. A guarded airship called [Resolute](../places/resolute.md) has arrived, and nearby ships offer pursuit as well as a possible escape route. No ship is seized, body recovered, main battle fought or person resurrected during Session 6.[^s006-end]
