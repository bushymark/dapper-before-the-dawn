# Airship confrontation

The Citizens are aboard a living-wood airship, called [Whipperwill](../places/wickerwill.md). The ship’s spelling is confirmed.[^s003-name-correction] Its blind captain, transcribed as Bernard/Berner/Burner, favors a traditional wheel and claims it gives him a feel for the living vessel. Three children travel with them. Masked boarders, affected by invisibility, emerge from below.[^opening]

Cole strikes the captain with an armored elbow, apparently aiming to knock him unconscious. The captain falls bleeding and is later described as dying before being healed. Armed pages, binders and an archivist respond. Cole’s attempt to rip a binder’s scroll away carries the wearer with it; the scroll ignites/explodes in the narrated exchange. The victim’s final fate is not confirmed.[^fight]

Maren pushes combatants apart using wind, while the Drifters voice a desire to spare children and nonmilitary people. Attempts to pilot initially fail and the ship banks dangerously. Koris calls forth the crocodilian companion **Swamp Poppy** to protect the children, but it attacks Cole despite instructions not to hurt anyone. Its face seems to melt and reform against his heat.[^fight]

The [White Fang](../places/white-fang.md) arrives above them with General Maochao and more personnel. Emeric casts Calm: the boarding force is suppressed, but the general resists. Emeric also heals Cole. Children are moved toward shelter below. Maren notices a familiar light growing in the sky.[^reinforcements]

Maren shoves Swamp Poppy off Cole, then tries a psychic attack. The GM describes finding no centralized brain, only something of vast scale compared with Maren; psychic immunity is ruled in this encounter. This does not independently identify the companion’s cosmic nature.[^psychic]

Dayne eventually stabilizes the vessel and takes up a briefcase as an improvised weapon. A binder’s scroll forms suspended paper stairs and empowers the descending general. Maochao aggressively attempts to disarm Cole; a later correction says it was a strong attempt, not a completed disarm. Emeric weakens the general and passes Cole a sword. Haskel heals the captain, who asks where the prisoner is and tells the rescuer to stop him. Elias enters the machinery area and activates a blue-glowing feline construct.[^turning]

# The ship breaks; rescue takes priority

A lore check recalls heavenly guardian beasts descending as comets in connection with new cities. This is recalled lore, not proof of who aimed the approaching object. A **car-sized prismatic egg-like object bisects the airship**, causing it to fall with passengers on separated sections. The theft fight becomes a rescue crisis.[^impact]

Some pages and archivists tumble away toward the city; their individual deaths or recoveries are not established. Cole assesses who seems afraid of death, unsuccessfully tries to push Maochao away, and jumps to another group, dropping his weapons and asking to save lives. Dayne makes a difficult piloting maneuver to redirect the wreck away from the metropolis toward the northern forest, then helps Koris across the gap. Elias works near the damaged engines.[^rescue]

Koris leaves treasured bags with another person and attempts a dangerous jump back toward the children. Maren’s powerful updraft helps her reach them. She reassures the children about their victus objects, but the child called **Prince Finn** says his crown is missing. A recollection says the professor dropped it toward Lower Lastings earlier. Its recovery remains unknown.[^children]

[Apherion](../characters/apherion.md) offers help[^s003-name-correction] and a cost is acknowledged, without the cost being clearly known. The three children grow radiant feathered wings and cross the gap with Koris. Apherion later warns that Elias’s plan will kill them; intervention is accepted. A connection to Emeric is apparent; its nature and the exact bargain remain unresolved.[^s003-name-correction][^intervention]

# Death and continued awareness

Elias reroutes the convergence engine and his device, hoping to expand a time-displacement effect over the engine room just before impact. The narration says the power is insufficient. He accepts a fallback jump in place as well as time, and teleportation triggers.[^engine]

The characters’ bodies dissolve into rising skin, muscle and blood. **Each of them knows they have died.** They pass imagery of a red gemstone and a wax tree with countless candles, then retain awareness in a beach-like, star-filled expanse without ordinary shape or mass, where time feels stopped. Apherion tethers their souls.[^s003-name-correction][^shore]

Seven apparent character-forms are represented by a gauntlet, tasselled cloak, armor, hat, compass, ring and inscribed clockwork object. The GM then adds four omitted forms: a crown, snow globe, little frog statue and clay figure of a red-haired woman.  Not every form’s identity is clear, and the group’s death does not yet establish a permanent fate. Independent movement and thought are explicitly described; ordinary speaking is not established at this point.[^shore]

# The cosmic passage

The passage’s exact duration, the whale’s discovery and some transitions remain unknown.[^gap]

# Elias’s return plan

Elias appears to know something about this place and proposes a method to return the group either to the material plane or to a state in which resurrection would be possible. **His theory requires sufficient elemental mass, speed and a focus.** Its effectiveness is not yet established.[^s003-gap-recollection]

Everyone contributes a piece of themselves to Elias, who begins shaping it and inscribing runes to craft the focus/foci. Whether these contributions are literally pieces of their souls remains uncertain. Amounts, permanence, consequences and any relationship to Apherion’s earlier price are not established.[^s003-gap-recollection]

The agreed plan is to use the remains of Whipperwill, harpoon the whale to gain speed, and have Elias use the crafted focus. Spellcasters would generate **fire, water, lightning and earth** to supply the necessary elemental mass. At the end of Session 3, successful generation of every element, execution of the method, return to the material plane or resurrection is not reported.[^s003-gap-recollection]

# The whale-towed departure

At the next known point, the group is discussing a boat and an astral whale. Dayne shapes a harpoon from starlight; another participant communes with the living boat about its purpose, flying. The boat dislikes the name **Whipperwill** and wants a new name; no replacement is recorded. Haskel forms a starlight chain attached to the harpoon. The group departs across the cosmic sky with the whale towing the vessel. Session 3 ends here; the destination and a return to ordinary life remain unknown.[^ending]

# State for the next session

- No successfully stolen, operational ordinary-world airship is established: the vessel was bisected and the group’s apparent death/transition followed.
- The groups cooperate to protect children and survive, but individual trust and a long-term alliance remain uncertain.
- Ordinary inventories, dropped weapons, bags, the prince’s crown, and other object-forms must not be treated as recovered simply because corresponding imagery appears later.
- The guardian-beast explanation, Swamp Poppy’s nature, intervention cost, unrecorded transitions, the return plan’s effectiveness and destination remain open.
