# Wreckside recovery and a shared deadline

The party remains in its improvised physical bodies at the altered airship wreck. Its wooden frame is now burdened with modern metal, chrome and alloy piping. Alora Valley remains the likely destination of the missing bodies and possessions.[^s005-opening]

Equipment recovery produces losses as well as a few survivals. Maren retains rope and robe but loses access to potions, kits and staff. Cole finds a bent crowbar and lifting belt; his pike and shield are absent. Emeric’s healing supplies, gifted sword and orb are missing. Koris finds her enchanted satchel intact but emptied, her telescope absent from its undamaged case, and no tuition money or mother’s handkerchief. Several unlabelled inventory outcomes remain uncertain. Elias’s badly damaged robotic feline emerges from the bushes and settles near him. Its breathing-like motion is mechanical, not proof that it has become alive.[^s005-equipment]

Dayne comforts Koris. Emeric gathers the group, discloses the **roughly thirty-hour limit**, and says the gauntlet offered an unspecified way to extend it. He has accepted no further bargain and promises not to choose for everyone alone. Expiry effects remain unknown. Elias asks what the intervention changed; Emeric says the presence could not find their bodies and used available material instead.[^s005-deadline]

# Justice, resurrection and trust

The Citizens explain dawn resurrection, victus links and the possibility of restoring souls to recovered bodies. The Court can sever links during criminal investigation. The Drifters have no victus objects of their own and may require proper plot proximity; recovery is still theoretical.[^s005-resurrection]

Elias identifies himself as a former prisoner of **the Fall**, with a continuing ban on inventing new creations. The Fall petrifies people until society is expected to have changed enough for their return; loved ones may join them. A legal assessment distinguishes the Citizens’ bystander status from the Drifters’ airship seizure, notes Haskel’s possible exposure, and suggests Elias’s astral construction might be judged differently from ordinary forbidden invention. These are expectations, not convictions or sentences imposed now.[^s005-law]

The Drifters propose helping everyone recover bodies and belongings, then accepting blame for coercing the Citizens if questioned. The parties debate trust, the Court’s authority and the search for answers about the shattered sun. The offer does not establish that the Court accepts this account. They begin moving north.[^s005-trust]

# Into the forest

Haskel maintains low gravity while the group chooses its exploration activities. Cole tests carrying the wreck but the enormous vessel catches trees and would sacrifice speed and subtlety. They proceed on foot.[^s005-travel]

After five or six hours in the enchanted First Woods, Therica requests rest near a well. Half a dozen gaunt wolves lie nearby, too weak to attack effectively. They have been starving, dying and returning without food; passing soldiers have helped drive game away. Finding prey would take hours. The party gives them a wide berth and promises to come back to help.[^s005-wolves]

# The silent forge

An unfinished settlement surrounds a once-impressive forge. Its exhausted human smith, provisionally named **Recknar**, admits the group despite their alarming forms. Broken display cases, unused machinery and depleted stock show the collapse of a flourishing workshop. A moved rope with a loop, sleeplessness and malnourishment suggest a man preparing to end his life. He offers shelter, use of the forge and a cart; Alora lies about twelve hours north on foot.[^s005-smith]

The smith says the Bloom killed his family and the Court failed to protect them. He discourages interest in the heavily chained basement. Cole lights his hearth. Koris smells blood and senses a cursed object below, then confides in Haskel. Haskel invokes Court authority and demands an explanation; the smith reacts furiously but yields the key.[^s005-basement]

Below, two dead people hang shackled above a floor stained with blood. A curved, jagged blade with a snake-like hilt lies nearby. Koris tries to understand its repeated sense that it is “time” to kill again, wraps it without direct contact, and finds it too heavy for her doll form. The blade has a vicious magical purpose, without an independently demonstrated will commanding its bearer.[^s005-blade]

# Revenge against the messengers

Recknar says the Bloom’s leader once sought something below his mines with a flower-marked walking tree. Years later a wooden humanoid with the same markings returned, emerged alone from the depths, murdered his wife and two children, and named himself **Tallow**. The Court had promised protection but arrived weeks too late.[^s005-history]

The smith captured two Court messengers and killed them repeatedly for years to overstrain the resurrection plot at its edge. He says its reach has receded by miles, leaving the captives permanently dead and potentially exposing Court barracks beyond Alora. Their bodies and missing victus objects support the immediate horror; distant casualties are not established. Earlier killings had involved sedation and quicker heart wounds; the latest show less care. His revenge has struck messengers rather than Tallow himself.[^s005-revenge]

Koris entrusts the wrapped blade to Haskel. The Citizens insist it must be surrendered for destruction and fear additional consequences if it is handed to the Drifters. No one uses it. Cole hears angry voices aligned with the smith’s rage and offers vengeance if Tallow is ever encountered. A brief hope fades after an unsuccessful appeal. The party leaves with the cart and blade, choosing not to enter the mines. Recknar remains alive; his subsequent fate is unknown.[^s005-departure]

# What the plots cover

On the road, the group learns that a plot is a territory covered by resurrection magic, spreading outward from a city. Victus objects make return easier and can preserve recoverability longer, but do not automatically revive someone beyond the covered area. The Court keeps identifying records for citizens and their objects.[^s005-plots]

The magic is attributed to a Forged from Rios, provisionally named **Yan Savos**, who exchanged it for nine dreadnought-scale skyships. After testing the spell for two or three years, the Court opened its first plot. He departed into the astral sea and has not returned in the account given. The discussion separates this history from the players’ broader previous-campaign knowledge.[^s005-savos]

The cart allows the participants who need rest to ride while others pull. The parties continue debating science, authority, preparedness and their different histories. Therica gives Dochas Ùr’s population as **845**. Neither side’s argument becomes a settled verdict on the Court.[^s005-road]

# Smidgen’s refuge

The woods open into huge produce fields and a rural settlement. Near the Court’s ships and gathering, a gnome named **Smidgen**, the town’s **patchwork**, urgently calls the group inside. She splashes turpentine near her doorway and covers windows. She says the White Fang’s captain is holding a retirement celebration while refusing to rest until the pursued people are caught. Court shipments have brought corpses and possessions to the stage of a displaced theater troupe’s performance area.[^s005-smidgen]

The GM clarifies that **no dawn has occurred since the party’s return**, and its partial physical and magical relocation disrupted ordinary resurrection. Day and night lengths vary; the discussion does not establish an exact number of dawnless weeks.[^s005-dawn]

Smidgen conceals the guests from an archivist suitor, gives five sausage links for the wolves, and points out abandoned theater supplies. The party helps with household chores. She permits a short stay but wants them and any confrontation away from her home.[^s005-supplies]

# A play within a play

Cole and Maren propose impersonating performers and staging a flattering historical play about the general’s victories, using it to recover their bodies with minimal conflict. Smidgen says officials had already sought such a performance. Disguises, a diversion and an existing explosive design are discussed; Elias’s ban is clarified to allow reproducing existing inventions, not creating a new one.[^s005-heist]

Koris initially prefers surrender but reconsiders when reminded that her missing cursed objects might still need her help. Dayne voices support for the group. Debate over surrender and the precepts remains part of the decision, rather than proof that all legal concerns are resolved.[^s005-decision]

At session end, part of the patrol heads toward the forest. The general is visible at the celebration with a rapier; binders who have been working on victus objects and possessions break to join the festivities. **Eight dark obsidian, coffin-like containers** stand beside the stage, and a hand belonging to one of the party’s bodies is visible inside one. The heist remains unexecuted: no disguises worn, play performed, explosives used or bodies recovered.[^s005-end]
