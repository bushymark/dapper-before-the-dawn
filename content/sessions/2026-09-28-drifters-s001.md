# Recap of the captured material

## A child at the barrier

The narration opens with a discussion of tragedy, then stages an encounter fourteen years before the story's present. A child is sent alone through rain and mud toward the army outside Dochas Ùr's barrier, while the populace watches. Hail outside becomes rain as it crosses the barrier. The child tries to imitate a military father's calm bearing.[^transcript]

The [general](../characters/general-at-the-barrier.md) accuses the city's people of harming outsiders and invokes the [Court of Conscience](../factions/court-of-conscience.md). She asks for disarmament, trust, and for the child to cross to her side. The child challenges the arrival of an armed force as a gesture of friendship and remains on the city side.[^transcript]

The general claims the barrier is no obstacle if her forces choose to act, but her sword strike ricochets without breaking through. She asks that 200–300 residents travel back with the fleet for discussions. The child agrees to report her words and actions, maintaining that trust must be earned.[^transcript] These are the general's demands and assertions in the depicted scene, not confirmation of good intentions or of the truth of her accusations.

The sequence was presented to Maren as a dream. He experiences the narrated events within it and then wakes in the present day. It is deliberately unresolved whether this was his own memory, someone else's experience seen through the dream, or a dream without established historical truth. It is not confirmed that Maren was the child. This ambiguity belongs to the scene itself, rather than being merely a gap in the transcript.[^dream-dictation]

The transcript separately contains seven/nine age language. Do not apply the child's age or the narrated historical date to Maren's biography while the child's identity remains unknown.[^transcript]

## Maren’s festival morning

Maren wakes around noon in a small second-story apartment above a shop. Open windows have let in a strong breeze. He finds his cloak folded at his feet, dresses in his scholarly equipment and tasselled cloak, and wears a distinctive key on a chain. No agent or supernatural cause is established for the cloak's position.[^transcript]

The narration describes the city as a beloved patchwork of improvised building materials and upward expansion. Residents avoided building outside the wall because they did not want an excluded/slum district; later layers use increasingly unusual materials. The named hoard and some material details are garbled, so those specifics are not normalized into canon.[^transcript]

Today is a [remembrance festival](../events/remembrance-festival.md), welcoming travelers home. Kites help guide returning people toward the city. Sentimental metal objects and, where supplied, loved ones' ashes are forged into weapons, then remade into needed household items after the ceremonies. Maren is energized by seeing his home active and alive; he tucks the key into his shirt rather than contributing it.[^transcript]

## Cole intervenes

A festival master of ceremonies and his daughter pass through the city checking whether people need help. Nearby, [Sledge](../characters/sledge.md) argues with an [expectant father from Eok](../characters/eokian-expectant-father.md), pulls him into an alley, and pins him by the belt. [Cole Buckit](../characters/cole-buckit.md), described as a 9½-foot figure of flame, plasma, and lightning, follows and approaches to de-escalate. A Diplomacy roll is reported as a natural 20, total 30.[^transcript]

Sledge accuses the man of planning to kill his own child as a cultural ritual. Cole challenges the assumptions being made and gives the man a small desired trinket, carefully managing the difficulty of physically holding it. The narration says Cole's presence prevents escalation, but Sledge still refuses to let the man down.[^transcript]

The man claims the child would be brought back, the act provides protection, and his wife agrees. These are attributed claims, with important ritual details missing or garbled. The transcript ends during this discussion and some festival-name chatter.[^transcript]

**Supplementary user recollection:** Sledge is the man’s father-in-law and is outraged by the apparent plan to undertake a traditional birth ceremony in which the newborn would effectively be killed. The wife apparently agrees, her name was not recalled in that note and was later supplied as Saga.[^departure-dictation] Cole speaks with Sledge about strange practices in Dochas Ùr and Rios; the encounter ends peaceably, though Sledge is not necessarily happy. No performed ceremony or outcome for the child is established.[^sledge-dictation]

## Emeric’s introduction and the kite competition — supplementary recollection

Emeric was helping an unnamed child build a kite in the street to welcome the child's father home from a long voyage, as part of a kite-festival tradition in Rios. He encountered Cole and Maren and brought them over to help, uniting the party. All three assisted with construction. In the subsequent skill-based kite competition, they used combat maneuvers and kite piloting to help the child win with the last kite standing.[^kite-dictation]

This scene comes from later user recollection, rather than identifiable speech in the supplied transcript. No precise audio position or festival prize is established.

## The sun shatters and reforms — supplementary recollection

After the kite competition, a falling spark passed through the sun. In the scene as described to the party, the sun shattered into thousands of pieces across the sky, darkening the land. The object, also described as a comet, continued toward a distant crash in the sea beyond the party’s sight. Within a couple of seconds the sun’s fragments reassembled and daylight returned.[^sun-dictation]

**Unconfirmed recollection:** the user recalls that the reformed sun may have been further along its path, as though several hours had passed, despite the event lasting only seconds. This needs confirmation; neither an actual passage of hours nor a cause for the apparent movement is established.[^sun-dictation]

See [event record](../events/sun-shattering.md).

## Public fear and Therica’s summons — supplementary recollection

The people were terrified after the sun event. The user explains that Rios’s people are familiar with fantastical events and world-ending threats, and take such threats very seriously; the current campaign is twenty years after Campaign 1.[^therica-dictation]

After an unspecified interval, [Therica Ashtongue](../characters/therica-ashtongue.md) summoned the party. She recounted the history of their community’s relationship with the [Court of Conscience](../factions/court-of-conscience.md), recalling the Court’s appearance in Maren’s dream that morning, and began outlining a request for a journey. The destination and purpose are supplied in the next dictated note; terms remain unknown; later dictation supplies the party’s acceptance.[^departure-dictation][^therica-dictation] The connection does not establish that Maren was the child or that the dream was his own memory.

Therica was identified as the former Highhelm king’s daughter, with a condition described as a severe cold-iron allergy and a grayscale appearance on parts of her face, compared by the user to “Morgane” from Campaign 1.[^therica-dictation] See [Therica’s record](../characters/therica-ashtongue.md) for spelling provenance and limits of that comparison.

## Therica’s airship investigation proposal — supplementary recollection

Therica proposed crossing the ocean to **Eok**, to the city called **Lastings** in the dictation, and asking the Court of Conscience’s leadership to lend, sell or otherwise provide access to an airship. She explained that Rios lacks airship technology while the Court has it. Her goal was to obtain airship access or technology so the party could investigate the sun event and the unidentified comet/meteor, and assess a possible threat to Dochas Ùr, Rios or the planet.[^airship-dictation]

The user identifies Dochas Ùr’s **Pillars** as the original adventuring party from Campaign 1. In Therica’s briefing, accounts attributed to the Pillars described flying much higher than expected, suggesting that the world’s apparent sky, roof or ceiling might be reachable by airship. This is reported evidence and an inference behind the proposed investigation; no sky boundary or airship reaching it has been directly established.[^airship-dictation]

See [proposed mission](../quests/airship-investigation.md). The following note supplies acceptance, companions and departure.

## Acceptance, companions and departure briefing — supplementary recollection

The party accepted Therica’s mission. Emeric persuaded her to accompany them with a very high roll, and she agreed immediately; neither the exact roll nor the skill used is supplied.[^departure-dictation]

Therica feared for her position and the leadership of Dochas Ùr. She expected House Glaren to make a bid for leadership while the party was away and thought it likely the House would be in charge upon their return. This is her prediction, not an accomplished takeover. The user identifies Sygar as an ostracized son of that House who renounced his family and became one of the original Pillars.[^departure-dictation] House Glaren’s spelling remains provisional because this is software dictation.

Nataniel and his very pregnant wife **Saga**, Sledge’s daughter, joined the journey; Nataniel, originally from Eok, would provide information. The party left almost immediately aboard a vessel captained by **Silvestro Casetti**. The user recalls little preparation beyond Maren doing further research; its subject and results are not recalled here.[^departure-dictation]

Before departure, the party was told that Eok has advanced technology and many inventors, scientists and geniuses, and that there are nine precepts to follow. The incomplete briefing included a relationship between eye color and which kinds of magic people can cast, “never eat fish” and “never lie.” The source of this briefing, precise wording and scope, exceptions, and the full set of nine precepts are not supplied. These are reported statements, not established universal magical laws or a complete code.[^departure-dictation]

## Sea journey, former Howl boundary and Eok testimony

Captain Silvestro Casetti spoke of Titan’s Gates, reportedly built by Titans and found by heroes who fought against the figure dictated as “Vale.” A check concerning what Titans are failed; no further identification was obtained. The skill, roller and numerical result are not supplied. In this prior-campaign context, the dictated “Vale” is matched to [Vaeldraxin (Vael)](../characters/vaeldraxin.md), whose short name and role are now user-confirmed.[^vaeldraxin-correction][^sea-dictation]

During the sea journey, the vessel crossed the area where the Howl had formerly encircled Rios; the user says it was brought down in Campaign 1. Emeric and Maren had distinct experiences there.[^sea-dictation]

Emeric perceived enormous creatures standing or walking in the sea, and one appeared to look at him. This records his perception; it does not establish that everyone saw the creatures, that they were physically present, or that they were Titans.[^sea-dictation]

Maren experienced an overwhelming, incoherent sensory assault: an army-like presence spreading before him, seeming to ingest or invade him, with voices, pleas and cries. It seemed to branch through him like a tree. These are experiential descriptions, not an established literal transformation or identified source.[^sea-dictation]

Nathaniel gave an unreliable, fragmentary overview of Eok: millions of inhabitants compared with perhaps thousands at home, phylactery-like victus objects, arcane-plot resurrection, possible refusal to return after death, a famous woman from Maren’s dream, a Court adventurers’ guild, sacred fish and sky, eye-color/magic restrictions, truthfulness and apparent military service at ten with renewal at eighteen. See the attributed account for details and uncertainty.[^sea-dictation]

## Arrival, Court leadership and city observations

Upon arrival, the party asked to speak with **Therus**, the current leader of the Court of Conscience. The user identifies him as **Paedan Dahhm’s son and successor**: Paedan had led the Court previously and had interacted with the Pillar Morgaen many years earlier. This establishes the recalled succession and relationship, not that the party had already met Therus.[^arrival-dictation]

The party was told there are thousands of gods, in contrast with the relatively few known in Rios. The speaker and exact geographical scope are unspecified; this is a reported claim, not an independently counted pantheon.[^arrival-dictation]

The destination city, previously called Lastings, had upper and lower levels. While exploring, the party saw humans and other species in the upper level but noticed no orcs; they saw dwarves below but no elves. These are observations from the party’s route, not a complete census or proof of exclusion rules.[^arrival-dictation]

The party was introduced to **General Maochao**, a rakshasa who captained an airship. This introduction does not yet establish that his airship was offered, acquired or boarded.[^arrival-dictation]

See [city record](../places/lastings.md).

## Therus’s audience and return under restrictions

The audience was granted almost immediately. The party was led to the strange ship where Therus resided. He was wheeled out in a black cube, with a fetus-like figure visible at its center. The party was knocked unconscious and then communicated with him in what appeared to be another plane or a mental space; its nature is unknown. There, he appeared as an angelic winged being. His presentation does not establish an angelic ancestry or that the fetus-like image describes his literal developmental state.[^audience-dictation]

During the conversation, the party understood Therus to be a child who hated his parents and Rios, resented Paedan’s affection for Rios, and disliked the party. They learned that his parents were serving time and that he had turned them in, condemning them before becoming the Court’s de facto leader. This records the information obtained and the party’s assessment during the audience; the charges, sentence, exact age and parents’ own account are not supplied.[^audience-dictation]

Therus told the party not to fear the falling object, identifying it as a **guardian beast**. He described these as common in Eok, falling from the sky to defend people, and treated information about them as sacred knowledge. The recollection is unclear whether the protected people were in Rios or Eok, and whether they were said to hatch from “tears” or “fears.” These are Therus’s claims; the object’s identity, origin and protective purpose are not independently confirmed.[^audience-dictation]

Therus could hear the party’s conversations through their shared mental link during the encounter. Whether he could also hear that link outside the strange encounter space is explicitly unknown; no persistent surveillance or universal telepathic ability is inferred.[^audience-dictation]

The user recalls Therus becoming petulant, refusing questions or leeway, rejecting the party and casting them out of the encounter. General Maochao then escorted them back to their ship and conveyed severe restrictions: they could not freely go places, speak to people, explore or investigate, with military accompaniment required. The party was left with no apparent diplomatic route to airship access or independent investigation at that point. This does not establish permanent impossibility, a specific legal sentence, or the duration and exact exceptions of the restrictions.[^audience-dictation]

See [audience record](../events/therus-audience.md).

## Attempting to seize an airship — session ending

After returning to their ship, the party concluded that there was no apparent peaceful route forward. Cole began challenging military personnel to duels. The party assessed these soldiers as exceptionally weak, lacking real weapons and unable to threaten them meaningfully; this is their assessment of the encountered personnel, not a measured claim about all Eok forces.[^ending-dictation]

Seeing an airship being loaded at the docks, the party fought its way into the airship’s hold and attempted to take it by force. **Session 1 ended during this combat.** Successful seizure, control of the vessel, casualties and the battle’s resolution are not established. The airship is not identified as Maochao’s vessel.[^ending-dictation]
